Conclusions

  1. With Na-ion prices, the baseline cell costs $52.63/kWh, about $25 below the 2025 Na-ion market midpoint. The gap points to costs the model does not yet capture, not to a real advantage.
  2. Non-material OpEx is scaled from material cost with a Li-ion ratio (73%). Cheaper Na-ion materials therefore also shrink labor, energy and overhead, which is not physical.
  3. Process scrap and energy density move cost more than any single price. Solid-state lines with higher ramp-up scrap will be penalized most.
  4. The liquid electrolyte is now a small cost line ($4.04/kWh, 13% of materials). Solid and gel electrolytes will be judged mainly by their processing (sintering, dry room, H₂S handling) and scrap, not by salt price.
  5. CapEx is fixed at 70 M$/GWh, so plant scale has no effect on unit cost.
